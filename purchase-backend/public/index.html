What SnapIT does differently.

Whole-system scan

Every drive you own.

Point SnapIT at your phone, your laptop, the NAS in the cupboard, the external drive in the drawer. It reads every image and every video on every one of them and files them together — deduplicated, dated, organised — in a single library on the storage you pick.

Events, not folders

Sorted by story.

Photos already know when they were taken, where, on what camera. SnapIT clusters them into events the way you'd remember them — the weekend away, the kitchen renovation, the birthday — and gives you a browser that reads like a story of the year, not a filesystem tree.

Netflix-style library

Built for the pile.

Ten years of camera roll, one wall of tiles: rows for years, rows for people, rows for places, rows for events. Photos and videos in the same grid. Duplicates folded automatically. Nothing to name, nothing to file by hand.

What we're building right now.

Now Whole-system source graph. Reading every drive, phone backup and cloud-takeout dump you point at, resolving the same file across all of them so nothing gets duplicated when it's actually the same photo on three devices.
Now Video support. Every format phones and cameras write, thumbnails from the first real frame, filed side-by-side with photos in the same library. Playback in the app, no external player required.
Now Metadata repair. WhatsApp and most messengers strip the story out of every photo they send. SnapIT reads the filename patterns, folder context and temporal neighbours to put the date, place and event back — and always says when a field was inferred vs. read.
Next Event clustering. Break the timeline into the events a human would remember — the trip, the birthday, the site visit — using time, place, camera and burst structure. Every event gets its own row in the browser.
Next Netflix-style browser. The R·01 grid becomes rows: Year, People, Places, Events, Recent, Highlights. Every row scrolls independently, big posters, keyboard-only navigable, same visual language as our video studio.
Next Face grouping. On-device face embeddings, cluster the same person across a decade of photos. Nothing leaves the machine. Fully opt-in — off by default, one toggle to enable, one to erase the whole face index.
